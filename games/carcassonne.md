# Carcassonne

> A tile-placement game of building cities, roads, monasteries, and fields for points.

## Goal  

Score the most points by building structures using tiles and meeples.

## Setup  

- 2–5 players  
- Place the starting tile face up  
- Shuffle all remaining tiles face down  
- Each player gets 7 meeples (followers)  

## How to Play  

On your turn, perform the following steps in order:

### 1. Draw & Place a Tile  

- Draw one tile from the deck  
- Place it adjacent to existing tiles  
- Edges must match:
  - City to city  
  - Road to road  
  - Field to field  

### 2. Place a Meeple (Optional)  

- You may place **1 meeple** on the tile you just placed  
- It can be placed on:
  - City  
  - Road  
  - Monastery  
  - Field  

#### Placement Rule  
- You **cannot place a meeple** on a feature that is already occupied by another meeple  
- This applies even if the feature becomes connected later  

### 3. Score Completed Features  

Score immediately when a feature is completed:

- **City** → 2 points per tile (plus shields if present)  
- **Road** → 1 point per tile  
- **Monastery** → 9 points when fully surrounded  

- After scoring, the meeple used is returned to the player  

### Fields (Farms)  

- Fields are **not scored during the game**  

At the end of the game:

- Each completed city adjacent to a field = **3 points**  
- Meeples placed in fields remain there until the end  

## End of Game  

The game ends when all tiles have been placed.

Final scoring:

- Incomplete features still score (usually at reduced value)  
- Fields are scored  

## Win Condition  

The player with the **highest total score** wins.

## Easy Way to Remember  

Place tile → Place meeple → Complete features → Score

## Strategy  

- Don’t overcommit meeples—running out is a common mistake  
- Early game: focus on quick, small completions for steady scoring  
- Cities with shields offer high value but carry more risk  
- Block opponents by disrupting their large structures  
- Fields are powerful late-game but require long-term planning
